# Lindt & Sprüngli: business activities and routes to market

**Scope and approach.** This is a group-level overview based primarily on Lindt & Sprüngli’s 2025 Integrated Annual Report and its company website. “All” here means the activities the company identifies as part of its business or value chain—not every internal corporate function or supplier relationship. The Group describes itself as focused on chocolate, but its business also includes related confectionery, its own retail and café operations, and a responsible-cocoa program. **The Group does not publish a complete country-by-country list of where every product or service is sold.** Where that information is not stated, I say so rather than infer it. ([lindt-spruengli.com](https://www.lindt-spruengli.com/investors))

## 1. Chocolate and related confectionery

- **Products:** Premium chocolate bars, pralines, truffles, filled chocolates, seasonal and gift assortments, and other chocolate-related confectionery. The Group’s brands include Lindt, Ghirardelli, Russell Stover, Whitman’s, Pangburn’s, Caffarel, Hofbauer and Küfferle. Some product lines include sugar-free confectionery. ([lindt-spruengli.com](https://www.lindt-spruengli.com/amfile/file/download/id/10512/file/Integrated-Annual-Report-2025.pdf))
- **Direct buyers:** Mainly retail customers—such as supermarkets and other retail partners—and distributors. Consumers also buy directly through Lindt & Sprüngli’s own shops and e-shops. Some branded products are sold through travel retail. The company describes wholesale as its primary distribution route, supplemented by its own retail and digital channels. ([reports.lindt-spruengli.com](https://reports.lindt-spruengli.com/sustainability-report-2024/strategy-and-governance/strategy/our-business-model-and-value-chain.html))
- **Ultimate consumers:** Individual consumers buying for personal consumption, gifts and seasonal occasions. ([reports.lindt-spruengli.com](https://reports.lindt-spruengli.com/sustainability-report-2024/strategy-and-governance/strategy/our-business-model-and-value-chain.html))
- **How sold:** Through retail partners’ physical stores and online channels; the Group’s own shops and e-shops; distributors, particularly in markets where it has no local entity; and travel retail. The company reported sales in **around 120 countries**. Lindt products specifically are available in more than 120 countries. ([reports.lindt-spruengli.com](https://reports.lindt-spruengli.com/sustainability-report-2024/strategy-and-governance/strategy/our-business-model-and-value-chain.html))
- **Countries, especially EEA:** The company’s 2025 reporting identifies a Europe business segment, but that is not a country-by-country product-sales list. It does **not** state in the sources reviewed the complete set of countries where each brand or product is sold, nor provide a complete EEA-only list. **Countries of sale for each product are not stated.** ([lindt-spruengli.com](https://www.lindt-spruengli.com/investors/integrated-annual-report-2025/))

## 2. Chocolate manufacturing and cocoa-mass production

- **Product or service:** The Group manufactures finished chocolate and, for most of its business, makes its own cocoa mass from sourced beans as part of its “bean-to-bar” production model. Its website describes processing cocoa beans into cocoa mass and then into chocolate products. Russell Stover and Ghirardelli are exceptions to the Group’s cocoa-mass production approach: they purchase chocolate mass or finished chocolate products. ([lindt-spruengli.com](https://www.lindt-spruengli.com/company/from-bean-to-bar/))
- **Direct buyers:** Finished chocolate products are sold to retailers, distributors and consumers through the channels above. The company’s public sources reviewed do not establish that cocoa mass is sold as a standalone product to unrelated third-party manufacturers.
- **Ultimate consumers:** Consumers of the finished chocolate products.
- **How sold:** The company says it has 12 production sites in Europe and the United States and distributes products through retail partners, its own shops and websites, and distributors. ([reports.lindt-spruengli.com](https://reports.lindt-spruengli.com/sustainability-report-2024/strategy-and-governance/strategy/our-business-model-and-value-chain.html))
- **Countries:** Production locations are stated at regional level—Europe and the United States—not as a complete country list on the cited overview. The company reports sales in around 120 countries, but does not state a complete product-by-product sales-country list. ([lindt-spruengli.com](https://www.lindt-spruengli.com/company/at-a-glance/))

**Scope:** I treat “Ritter Sport” primarily as **Alfred Ritter GmbH & Co. KG and its food-business operations**, not automatically as every enterprise owned by members of the Ritter family. ## Important limits on the country inventory

The strongest named-market evidence concerns the **FRoSTA consumer brand** and its **own online shop**. The company does **not** publish a comprehensive activity-by-country matrix for private label, industrial supply, home-delivery suppliers, consulting or its smaller brands in the sources reviewed. Its statement that it operates with employees in seven European countries is an operational footprint—not a complete sales-country list. Likewise, production in Germany and Poland does not imply sales are restricted to those countries. ([frosta-ag.com](https://www.frosta-ag.com/dokumente/jahresfinanzbericht-frosta-konzern-2025-2/?tmstv=1791938771))

Consequently, this is an inventory of **all disclosed commercial activities found**, with explicit gaps—not a claim that every country of sale or every customer relationship is publicly identifiable.
